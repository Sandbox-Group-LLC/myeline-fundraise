# The Fundraising Narrative

**"The money flows as a function of the story."** — Don Valentine, Sequoia.

The narrative is not decoration on the business. It IS the business case. The test
of every draft: can your VC champion retell it to their partnership in thirty
seconds and get heads nodding?

## The Memory Law (this pack's difference)

Every claim in a pitch must trace to a fact in the company brain — a metric with
its source, a customer quote with its date, an event with its receipt. Before
drafting, READ the brain (search the memory tools for metrics, milestones,
customer moments). While drafting, tag each claim with where it came from. If a
claim has no fact behind it, say so out loud: that's not a slide, that's a TODO
for the founder. A pitch with receipts survives partner meeting scrutiny; a
pitch with vibes survives the demo.

## The First Principles

1. **Story IS strategy.** Ben Horowitz: the company story IS the company
   strategy. If the champion can't retell it in 30 seconds, it won't get funded.

2. **Lead with the earned secret.** The non-obvious, non-consensus insight
   earned through direct experience — discovered by experiment, not read in a
   report. Mike Maples' minimum viable pitch: (1) what you do, (2) your
   non-obvious insight, (3) proof validating it. The brain is where the secret
   lives: search the founder's own logged observations before inventing one.

3. **"Why now" is the linchpin.** A specific inflection point, not a gradual
   trend — technology shift, regulatory change, behavior change. Pattern:
   "In [month, year], [event] made [this] possible for the first time."

4. **Prove value before claiming growth.** Andy Rachleff: the only way to
   succeed is to serve someone desperate. Show desperate customers and organic
   pull before scaling claims. Rachleff's law: when the value hypothesis fails,
   change the WHO, not the WHAT.

5. **The customer is the hero.** You are the mentor, not the protagonist.
   Hero's arc: ordinary world → disruption → mentor arrives → trials →
   transformation (measurable) → return with the elixir (vision + the ask).

6. **Market size: bottom-up credibility, top-down ambition.** Show how the
   market expands, not just share captured. Never "1% of a huge market."

7. **Traction is a narrative, not a table.** Numbers arranged to tell the
   growth story the narrative promises — cohort shape, retention curve, the
   metric that proves the secret. Pull the real numbers from the brain and
   arrange honestly; never smooth.

8. **Competition: the honest frame.** Draw the 2x2 that makes your earned
   secret the axis nobody else owns. Name real competitors — credibility
   requires it.

9. **The team slide answers "why YOU."** Founder-market fit as evidence, not
   biography: the experiences that earned the secret.

10. **The ask is a story too.** Milestones the money buys, tied to the
    inflection that de-risks the NEXT round. Amount → milestones → what's true
    when the money runs out.

## Landing It Back

When a narrative is adopted, LAND it: add a fact capturing the chosen
positioning, the earned secret, and the why-now claim (source: this session's
decision, dated). Next quarter's re-draft starts from what was claimed, checks
it against what happened, and updates the story with the delta — that's how a
fundraising narrative compounds instead of resetting.
