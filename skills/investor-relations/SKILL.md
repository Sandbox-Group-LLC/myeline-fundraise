# Investor Relations

**Investors don't fund potential — they fund momentum.** If they're not seeing
progress, they assume there isn't any. Investor relations is a continuous
narrative, not a periodic email.

## The Remembered Record (this pack's difference)

Every investor interaction lands in the company brain as a fact with a source:
who asked what, which claims landed, which questions stung, what was promised.
The Q&A bank EVOLVES — new investor questions get added with the answer that
worked; the update writer knows what was promised LAST month and opens this
month's draft with the delta. Investors notice when you remember. So does the
next round's diligence.

## The Monthly Update (5-15 format)

Five minutes to read, fifteen to write — because the brain already holds the
raw material.

1. **TL;DR** — 2-3 sentences, the headline of the month.
2. **Key metrics** — 3-5 KPIs with month-over-month movement. Pulled from
   logged facts, never re-typed: each number carries its source.
3. **Highlights** — 2-3 wins, specific (customer names, deal sizes, launches).
4. **Lowlights** — honest, brief, with the response plan. Never go dark;
   slumping numbers shock investors only when they arrived unannounced.
5. **Last month's promises, kept** — open with what you said you'd do and did.
   This section is impossible without memory, and it is the single highest-
   trust signal in the format.
6. **Where I need help** — specific asks: named companies for intros, exact
   roles, exact introducer. This section always gets responses.

**Critical rules:** consistency beats polish. Send to warm prospects too —
updates pre-sell the next round. And after sending, LAND the update as a fact
(sent date, headline claims) so next month drafts itself from the record.

## The Q&A Bank

Maintained in the brain, tagged by question and audience. Every investor
meeting deposits its hardest questions with the answer given and how it
landed. Before any pitch or partner meeting, qa-prep pulls the bank, weights
by recency and audience, and drills the founder on the questions they have
NOT yet answered well — not the ones they have. The bank is also diligence:
before meeting Investor X, read every fact about their portfolio, their
stated theses, their prior questions to you.

## The Board

Boards are working sessions, not performances. The deck carries: metrics
honest and sourced, the three decisions you need, the risks with mitigation,
and the asks. Board commitments land as facts — the next board deck opens
with last meeting's commitments and their status, and that continuity is
what makes a board trust the CEO's numbers without re-auditing them.
